1. What This Product Is For

One place for onboarding, billing, payment review, and receipts

Setu Finance is designed to replace a fragmented workflow where client details live in one place, invoices in another, payment confirmations inside email, and exception handling inside team memory. The product creates one operational path from onboarding to invoice to payment application to receipt.

The most important product choice is that money does not get auto-posted blindly. The system captures and prepares transactions, but a human still completes the final apply step.

Business value

Reduce re-keying, missed follow-ups, and payment confusion across onboarding, invoicing, and Zelle-based collections.

Operational value

Give finance one queue for invoices, one queue for clear payments, and one queue for exceptions.

Future value

Stable customer IDs and append-only service history make the finance model reusable across future products.

3. Business Requirements

What the product must do

Client onboarding

  • The first step in the workflow must be client onboarding.
  • First name, last name, primary email, phone, and at least one enrolled service must be required.
  • Home address should be available as an optional field.
  • Every client must receive a stable customer ID.
  • Service history must preserve the date and time when each service was enrolled.

Customer search and record view

  • The customer register should feel dense and operational, similar to a finance worksheet rather than a loose card list.
  • Each customer row should show a clear status so finance can see whether the record is active, awaiting payment, overdue, or needs review.
  • Clicking a customer should open a 360 view with signup date, contacts, service history, invoices, payments, referrals, rewards, and working contract or billing information.

Invoices and billing

  • Finance users must be able to create invoices from the client record.
  • The invoice flow should reuse the client’s enrolled services as the default selection set.
  • The system must support invoice statuses such as draft, sent, paid, and overdue.
  • The system must support invoice email delivery.

Payments and transaction capture

  • The system must save synced Zelle payment confirmations as structured transaction records.
  • A transaction record should capture date, transaction number, amount, memo, source message details, and raw extracted content whenever available.
  • The matching engine should identify likely customer and invoice matches using saved customer information.
  • Clear matches go to a confirmation queue. Unclear cases go to an exceptions queue.
  • When a finance user resolves an exception, the system must preserve that decision as history, including the action taken, the resolving user, and the time of resolution.
  • When an existing customer is selected from an exception review, the saved transaction must immediately attach to that customer’s record before the final apply step.
  • A human must complete the final apply action.

Receipts and communication

  • Once a transaction is applied, the system must prepare a receipt for the customer’s primary email on file.
  • Applying the transaction and sending the receipt should remain separate actions so finance can re-send receipts later without re-posting money.
  • Receipts should be sent as a simple PDF attachment with key payment references such as amount, transaction number, memo, and applied date.
  • The system should keep enough transaction detail to explain later how and why the payment was applied.

Dashboard visibility

  • The dashboard should show received amounts over time in one simple chart.
  • Users should be able to switch that chart between day, week, month, and year views.
  • The x-axis should show date periods and the y-axis should show summed received amounts.
  • Duplicate-blocked transactions should stay out of the received totals.

Referral program

  • The default referral reward is $500, but admins must be able to change it.
  • The default qualification rule is $3,000 paid or 6 months, but admins must be able to change or disable it.
  • Historical referral relationships must preserve the rule snapshot active when they were created.
The portal should optimize for auditability, low operational friction, and future expansion into a broader suite rather than only solving a single invoice screen.
4. Simple Process Flow

How work moves through the portal

1
Onboard client
Capture required identity details, optional address, referral source, and initial service enrollments.
2
Create customer record
Assign a stable customer ID and preserve service history with timestamps.
3
Create and send invoice
Use the client’s saved services and contact details instead of re-keying the basics.
4
Capture payment
Bring in Zelle confirmation details from email or review the transaction manually.
5
Match customer and invoice
Use saved customer identity hints, services, and invoice context to prepare the best match.
6
Queue for review
Clear matches go to payments-to-confirm. Mismatches and ambiguities go to exceptions.
7
Human clicks apply
The operator completes the final payment posting with one click after reviewing the prepared record.
8
Completed transactions and receipt
The invoice is marked paid, the transaction moves into a completed ledger, and finance can send or re-send the PDF receipt while referral progress or rewards update safely.
5. Low-Cost AWS Solution Architecture

How this can be deployed without losing functionality

The target deployment keeps the same behavior while shifting the local prototype into a cost-conscious AWS footprint. The idea is to use managed services where they reduce operational burden, but avoid heavy fixed-cost infrastructure too early.

Portal frontend
Static web app hosted on Amazon S3 and delivered through CloudFront for fast HTTPS access.
low fixed cost
Application API
Node/Express service deployed on AWS App Runner so the current backend shape can move to AWS without a full rewrite.
simple ops
System of record
Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL stores customers, invoices, payments, exceptions, referral records, and activity history.
managed postgres
Background work
Amazon SQS and Lambda handle scheduled Gmail sync, transaction processing, and email jobs without requiring another always-on server.
usage-based
Outbound email
Amazon SES sends invoices and receipts at lower cost than most third-party vendors.
low per-message cost
Monitoring and secrets
CloudWatch handles logs and alarms, while Parameter Store and Secrets Manager keep credentials and tokens out of code.
managed controls
This target stack preserves login, onboarding, invoices, Gmail/Zelle sync, payment queues, one-click apply, receipts, and referral logic while keeping the first production shape relatively lean.
The product should stay intentionally simple at launch: single region, one primary app service, one Postgres database, and background jobs only where they materially improve reliability or response time.
6. Governance, Controls, and Policy

What should remain controlled

  • Finance data must stay behind login.
  • Money should not auto-post directly from an email sync without human review.
  • Transactions must remain saved even before they are applied.
  • Referral rules may change for future enrollments, but historical relationships must remain intact.
  • Customer IDs must remain stable so future products can reference the same customer cleanly.
7. What Is Intentionally Left For Later

Deferred scope

  • Public client self-service portal
  • Full bank API integration beyond the current Gmail/Zelle path
  • Automatic payment posting with no human review
  • Multi-tenant organization model for several businesses inside one deployment
  • Warehouse-grade analytics and business intelligence tooling
The current product already sets up the right foundation for those future steps, but it does not need their cost or complexity yet.
